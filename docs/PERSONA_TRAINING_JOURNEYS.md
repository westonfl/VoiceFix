# Rehear - Persona-Based 6-Month Training Journeys

This document describes how Rehear trains different beginner personas across months, weeks, and daily sessions.

The product direction is now:

> Rehear is a 6-month daily vocal training journey that helps beginners build the breath, resonance, control, confidence, and song skills needed to sing comfortably, using short recordings and repeatable feedback every day.

The app should still preserve the original Rehear strength: every training day uses a tight loop of **guided exercise -> record -> feedback -> one fix -> retry -> compare to yourself**.

---

## 1. The Universal 6-Month Arc

Every user sees the same broad journey, but the app changes exercise difficulty, pacing, feedback language, and skip recommendations based on their persona.

| Phase | Time | Main Goal | Training Focus | User Promise |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 | Month 0-1 | Build the instrument | Breath, gentle sound, resonance, comfort, recording confidence | "Your voice becomes steadier and easier to use." |
| Phase 2 | Month 1-3 | Build singing control | Pitch, intervals, short melodies, timing, reference fading | "Your ear, breath, and voice start working together." |
| Phase 3 | Month 4-6 | Apply technique to songs | Phrases, range, dynamics, expression, full takes, performance confidence | "Technique becomes music." |

Users can test out of early material, skip forward, or mix in songs, but the default recommendation should strongly favor this order.

---

## 2. Daily Session Chassis

Every daily session should feel familiar. The contents change, but the rhythm stays stable.

| Step | Purpose | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Arrival | Reconnect user to the journey | "Day 18 of 180 - Foundation: steady endings." |
| 2. Motivation | One short, serious reminder | "Small honest repetitions are how the voice changes." |
| 3. Warm-up drill | Make sound easy before training | Hum, hiss, lip trill, soft vowel |
| 4. Main drill | Train today's skill | Sustain, pitch match, interval, phrase |
| 5. Apply drill | Use the skill in a more musical context | Two-note echo, short lyric phrase, song fragment |
| 6. Feedback loop | Record, observe, retry | "The ending faded. Try a smaller sound." |
| 7. Summary | Show progress and preserve habit | Streak, best take, tomorrow preview |

Most sessions should take 5-15 minutes. Longer users can receive extra repetitions, not more conceptual clutter.

---

## 3. Core Personas

Rehear should train at least six beginner personas differently.

| Persona | Main Problem | Emotional Risk | Default Start |
|---|---|---|---|
| Asha - Air Runs Out | Fading endings, breath instability, wobble | Feels physically incapable | Phase 1, Week 1 |
| Jordan - Pitch Is Hard | Cannot find or hold target pitch | Thinks they are tone deaf | Phase 1 or Phase 2 placement check |
| Riley - Strains Higher | Pushing, tightness, cracking | May train harmful habits | Phase 1 with safety/tension path |
| Sam - Mixed Beginner | Everything is somewhat unstable | Overwhelm | Phase 1 foundation mix |
| Morgan - Nervous Recorder | Embarrassed, quiet, avoids playback | Shame and dropout | Phase 1 confidence path |
| Taylor - Musician Ear, Weak Voice | Hears issues but cannot execute | Frustration, impatience | Phase 1 accelerated or Phase 2 early |

## 4. Persona Asha - Air Runs Out

### Profile

Asha's voice fades at the end of notes. Sustained sounds become shaky, quiet, or flat. She may think she "has no breath support," but Rehear should avoid overclaiming and instead say: "Your endings often lose steadiness."

### Month-by-Month Training

| Month | Focus | What Rehear Trains | What Progress Looks Like |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 0-1 | Steady airflow and endings | Hiss, soft hum, sustained ah, small volume control | Endings fade less; user can hold simple sounds calmly |
| 1-2 | Pitch on stable air | Single pitch match after airflow warm-up | Pitch holds better when volume stays steady |
| 2-3 | Short melodic movement | Two-note and three-note echoes | Second and third notes stop collapsing |
| 4 | Song phrase endings | Short lyric lines with controlled exits | Last words stay present |
| 5 | Dynamics and expression | Soft/loud changes without dumping air | Can sing quietly without disappearing |
| 6 | Full short song takes | Compare Week 1 baseline to current phrase | User hears stronger, steadier endings |

### First Month Weekly Plan

| Week | Theme | Daily Training Emphasis |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Smaller sound, steadier air | Hiss -> soft hum -> sustained ah |
| 2 | Endings | Hold sounds through the final third without pushing |
| 3 | Air into pitch | Match one comfortable note after a breath drill |
| 4 | Air into tiny melody | Two-note connections and phrase endings |

### Daily Session Example

1. Motivation: "A steady ending is more useful than a loud beginning."
2. Warm-up: 10-second soft hiss.
3. Main drill: Sustain "ah" for 5 seconds.
4. Feedback: "The volume dipped near the end."
5. One fix: "Try a smaller sound, like a slow leak of air."
6. Retry: Same sustained "ah."
7. Apply: Two-note connection.
8. Summary: "Your second take held the ending slightly longer."

## 5. Persona Jordan - Pitch Is Hard

### Profile

Jordan struggles to find the right note. Breath may be acceptable, but target matching is unreliable. This user may believe they are "tone deaf," so Rehear must be precise and encouraging.

### Month-by-Month Training

| Month | Focus | What Rehear Trains | What Progress Looks Like |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 0-1 | Pitch readiness | Gentle hum, one-note matching, stable onset | User learns to approach notes without panic |
| 1-2 | Single-note accuracy | Reference tone -> hum -> ah | Faster target finding |
| 2-3 | Intervals and reference fading | Two-note patterns, then delayed reference | Can repeat small intervals with less guidance |
| 4 | Short melody fragments | 3-5 note patterns from simple songs | User recognizes melodic shape |
| 5 | Singing without constant guide | Listen once, then sing back | Less dependency on reference audio |
| 6 | Song pitch confidence | Verse/chorus fragments, before/after takes | User stays closer to melody across phrases |

### First Month Weekly Plan

| Week | Theme | Daily Training Emphasis |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Find one note | Reference tone, hum, open to vowel |
| 2 | Hold one note | Reduce sliding after finding target |
| 3 | Repeat the same target | Same note twice, compare attempts |
| 4 | Tiny pitch movement | Two-note echo patterns |

### Daily Session Example

1. Motivation: "Pitch is coordination, not a character judgment."
2. Warm-up: Gentle hum on any comfortable note.
3. Main drill: App plays one tone; user hums it.
4. Feedback: "You started slightly below the target, then moved closer."
5. One fix: "Hum first, then open to ah without changing pitch."
6. Retry: Same target note.
7. Apply: Two-note echo.
8. Summary: "You found the second attempt faster than the first."

---

## 6. Persona Riley - Strains Higher

### Profile

Riley can sing some easy notes but pushes when the range rises. The app must prioritize safety and avoid rewarding strained high notes.

### Month-by-Month Training

| Month | Focus | What Rehear Trains | What Progress Looks Like |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 0-1 | Comfort and release | Hums, lip trills, mid-range slides, stop-if-pain checks | User learns easy sound before range |
| 1-2 | Smooth movement | Small steps in comfortable range | Less cracking on tiny intervals |
| 2-3 | Controlled range expansion | Gradual semitone increases with comfort checks | Range grows without force |
| 4 | Song phrases in safe range | Transposed phrases, no high-note chasing | Can sing real phrases comfortably |
| 5 | Dynamic control | Softer high approaches, vowel modification basics | Less squeezing at phrase peaks |
| 6 | Performance stamina | Short songs with range management | User knows when to adjust instead of push |

### First Month Weekly Plan

| Week | Theme | Daily Training Emphasis |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Easy sound only | Soft hum, comfort check, no high targets |
| 2 | Smooth middle voice | Small slides and two-note steps |
| 3 | Reduce pushing | Softer onset, smaller volume |
| 4 | First gentle climb | Tiny upward patterns with stop conditions |

### Daily Session Example

1. Motivation: "Range grows from ease, not force."
2. Warm-up: Soft hum in comfortable range.
3. Main drill: Small two-note step, mid-range only.
4. Feedback: "The top note got louder and less stable."
5. One fix: "Try the top note softer, not stronger."
6. Retry: Same two-note step.
7. Apply: Short comfortable phrase.
8. Summary: "You stayed smoother when the second take was smaller."

---

## 7. Persona Sam - Mixed Beginner

### Profile

Sam has no single dominant issue. Air, pitch, timing, and confidence are all somewhat weak. The danger is overwhelming them with too much feedback.

### Month-by-Month Training

| Month | Focus | What Rehear Trains | What Progress Looks Like |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 0-1 | Foundation sampling | Air, hum, one pitch, basic recording habit | App identifies strongest weak point |
| 1-2 | Primary weakness | More sessions tilt toward air, pitch, or tension | User sees one clear focus |
| 2-3 | Balanced control | Pitch + breath + timing in small drills | Fewer chaotic takes |
| 4 | Simple song fragments | Apply one skill to one phrase | User connects drills to music |
| 5 | Consistency | Repeat phrase over multiple days | Less random performance |
| 6 | Full beginner song path | Verse sections, chorus fragments, confidence | User can complete a simple song take |

### First Month Weekly Plan

| Week | Theme | Daily Training Emphasis |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Sample the basics | Hiss, hum, single pitch |
| 2 | Find the sticky point | Repeat the drill that shows the clearest issue |
| 3 | Build one lane | More air, pitch, or tension work based on data |
| 4 | Add tiny song-like movement | Three-note echo and short phrase endings |

### Daily Session Example

1. Motivation: "The first job is not to fix everything. It is to find the next useful thing."
2. Warm-up: Soft hiss.
3. Main drill: Single pitch match.
4. Feedback: "The pitch was closer after the sound became smaller."
5. One fix: "Start gently, then hold."
6. Retry: Same pitch.
7. Apply: Three-note echo.
8. Summary: "Rehear will keep watching whether pitch or air needs more attention."

## 8. Persona Morgan - Nervous Recorder

### Profile

Morgan is embarrassed by recording, may sing too quietly, stop early, or avoid playback. The product challenge is retention before technique.

### Month-by-Month Training

| Month | Focus | What Rehear Trains | What Progress Looks Like |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 0-1 | Recording safety and tiny wins | Very short hums, optional playback, private clips | User completes sessions without quitting |
| 1-2 | Gentle vocal control | Soft pitch matching and short sustains | User tolerates hearing small comparisons |
| 2-3 | Confidence through repetition | Same easy drills with visible improvement | Less fear before tapping record |
| 4 | First song fragments | Short private phrases, no public framing | User sings recognizable material |
| 5 | Before/after confidence | Saved clips, best take comparison | User hears improvement without shame |
| 6 | Performance readiness | Full private takes, optional share/export later | User can record a song phrase calmly |

### First Month Weekly Plan

| Week | Theme | Daily Training Emphasis |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Show up | 5-minute sessions, 5-10 second recordings |
| 2 | Finish tiny takes | Completion over quality |
| 3 | Optional comparison | Metrics first, playback optional |
| 4 | First confident repeat | Same safe drill until it feels normal |

### Daily Session Example

1. Motivation: "Today the win is returning, not proving yourself."
2. Warm-up: 5-second hum.
3. Main drill: Soft "ah" for 3 seconds.
4. Feedback: "You completed the take. That is the bar today."
5. One fix: "Try the same sound one more time, still quiet."
6. Retry: Same sound.
7. Apply: Optional second hum.
8. Summary: "Day complete. Playback stayed optional."

## 9. Persona Taylor - Musician Ear, Weak Voice

### Profile

Taylor can hear mistakes but cannot make the voice obey yet. This user may get bored if the app feels too basic, but still needs foundation.

### Month-by-Month Training

| Month | Focus | What Rehear Trains | What Progress Looks Like |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 0-1 | Fast foundation placement | Breath and phonation checks, then accelerated path if stable | User earns faster progression |
| 1-2 | Ear-to-voice connection | Pitch match, intervals, slow scale fragments | Execution catches up with hearing |
| 2-3 | Musical control | Timing, interval accuracy, reference fading | Can sing what they hear more reliably |
| 4 | Song phrase analysis | Compare intended melody to actual take | User gets precise practice targets |
| 5 | Style and expression | Dynamics, phrasing, tone color | Musicality enters the app experience |
| 6 | Performance polish | Full takes, weak-spot loops, expressive repeat | User refines instead of only correcting |

### First Month Weekly Plan

| Week | Theme | Daily Training Emphasis |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Placement and proof | Foundation checks, skip option if stable |
| 2 | Pitch execution | Match targets, then repeat without rushing |
| 3 | Intervals | Two-note and three-note patterns |
| 4 | Musical application | Tiny melody fragments and timing |

### Daily Session Example

1. Motivation: "Hearing it is step one. Repeating it cleanly is the training."
2. Warm-up: Gentle hum.
3. Main drill: Two-note interval.
4. Feedback: "You heard the second note, but the voice arrived late."
5. One fix: "Slow the pattern and prepare the second note before you sing it."
6. Retry: Same interval.
7. Apply: Three-note echo.
8. Summary: "Your timing improved when the pattern slowed down."

## 10. Weekly Structure Across All Personas

Each week should have one main theme. This prevents the app from feeling random.

| Day | Role | Typical Session |
|---:|---|---|
| Day 1 | Baseline | Record current version of the week's skill |
| Day 2 | Stabilize | Repeat easiest version with one cue |
| Day 3 | Retry and compare | Same drill, slightly clearer target |
| Day 4 | Small stretch | Add one difficulty step |
| Day 5 | Apply | Use skill in a short musical fragment |
| Day 6 | Review | Compare Day 1 and Day 6 |
| Day 7 | Recovery or checkpoint | Gentle session, placement check, or rest-aware practice |

The app should avoid making every day equally intense. A sustainable 6-month journey needs rhythm.

---

## 11. Monthly Checkpoints

Every month should end with a checkpoint, not just a streak celebration.

| Checkpoint | What It Measures | What User Sees |
|---|---|---|
| Month 1 | Can the user make sound more steadily? | "Your endings are more stable than Week 1." |
| Month 2 | Can the user match and hold simple pitch? | "You find the target faster now." |
| Month 3 | Can the user echo simple melodic movement? | "Your voice follows small patterns more reliably." |
| Month 4 | Can the user apply control to short phrases? | "Technique is starting to survive inside music." |
| Month 5 | Can the user repeat a phrase consistently? | "Your best takes are becoming less accidental." |
| Month 6 | Can the user complete a comfortable song take? | "You can now train a song with a clear process." |

Monthly checkpoints should include:

- first recording from that phase
- latest recording from that phase
- one written observation
- one next-month recommendation
- option to skip, repeat, or advance

## 12. Habit, Streaks, and Motivation

Rehear should be serious about daily training, but not cruel.

### Streak Rule

If the user misses a day, the daily streak resets to 0.

### Journey Rule

The 180-day journey does not reset. The user's training history remains intact.

### Recovery Copy

Use language like:

> The streak reset. The training did not. Start again today.

> Missing one day does not erase the voice you are building.

> The next repetition matters more than the missed one.

### Daily Notification Types

| Tone | Example |
|---|---|
| Gentle | "Your voice only needs a few honest minutes today." |
| Strict | "Training time. Keep the promise you made to yourself." |
| Coach-like | "Show up, record once, improve one thing." |
| Motivational | "Six months is built one small session at a time." |

Notifications should include the user's current phase when possible:

> Day 23 of 180 - Foundation. Today: steadier endings.

---

## 13. Important Product Rules

1. The roadmap is strong, but not rigid.
2. Users can skip, test out, or add song practice early.
3. Feedback must stay limited to one or two observations.
4. Every training day must include at least one retry.
5. The app compares the user to their own previous take, not an ideal singer.
6. Foundation should not feel like punishment. Add small musical applications early.
7. Safety language must stay humble: "likely," "often," "sounds like," and "stop if it hurts."
8. Long-term motivation should be serious and dignified, not cheesy.

---

## 14. Product Summary

Rehear should train different users through the same 6-month journey, but with different emphasis:

- Asha gets more airflow and ending stability.
- Jordan gets more pitch matching and reference fading.
- Riley gets comfort, release, and safe range expansion.
- Sam gets a balanced diagnostic foundation until one weak point becomes clear.
- Morgan gets shorter, emotionally safer sessions.
- Taylor gets faster progression and more musical precision.

The app's job is to make the user feel:

> I know where I am in the journey. I know what to practice today. I can hear one small thing changing.
